## Post 1

Men are good at maintenance. We change oil that has not failed yet. We seal decks against rain that has not fallen.

Then we take the friendships we would run into traffic for and put them on a maintenance schedule of never.

## Post 2

Old friendship runs on history the way a house runs on good bones. It is why the thing stands. It is not why the lights are on.

## Post 3

"We should get together" is not a plan. It is a pleasantry wearing a plan's clothes.

# LinkedIn Page

Men maintain everything they value — oil before it fails, decks before the rain, smoke detectors twice a year — and then put their closest friendships on a schedule of never.

The myth is that strong friendships shouldn't need upkeep. We don't apply that logic to anything else we respect.

The new field note is about the short, unromantic schedule that keeps a friendship running: a call on a normal Tuesday, a specific question, one honest sentence back, a plan with a date on it. [canonical link]
