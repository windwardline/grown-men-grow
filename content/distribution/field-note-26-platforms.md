# Bluesky

## Post 1

Somewhere around forty, a man quietly stops doing anything he can't already do. Nobody announces it. He just looks up one year and finds every hour of his week going to something he's been good at since dial-up.

## Post 2

The first night on a pottery wheel produces an ashtray, which was supposed to be a bowl. Then it gets better fast. Beginners improve faster than anybody alive, because they're starting from nothing and nearly everything counts. Experts improve by inches.

# LinkedIn Page

Most men past forty spend their working week on things they've been good at for twenty years. That's what mastery looks like, and it's a fair deal. It also means improving by inches, in ways only two other people could see.

The new field note is about what a man gives up when he stops being a beginner at anything: the fastest improvement he'll ever feel, and sometimes the ability to train the new hire, because he can't remember not knowing. It's straight about the cost of starting over in front of people, and about the men who don't have a free Tuesday night to spend making ashtrays. [canonical link]
