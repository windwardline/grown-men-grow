# Bluesky

## Post 1

A routine ground ball to short. Two hops, glove down, throw to first. A man makes that play a thousand times without thinking about it, which is the trouble, because the thousand-and-first is the one where the ball stays low and he doesn't.

## Post 2

When the scorer decides which runs count against the pitcher, he replays the inning as though the error had been an out. Once that imaginary inning reaches three outs, it's over. Everything after that is unearned. Everybody on the field keeps playing an inning that, on paper, already ended.

# LinkedIn Page

A shortstop boots a routine ground ball and hands the other team a fourth out. The scorebook gives it two characters, E6, and baseball's scoring rules do something unexpectedly kind with it: the scorer replays the inning as though the ball had been caught, and every run after that imaginary third out is marked unearned. The runs count. They just don't count against the pitcher.

So the rest of the inning gets played by people who didn't make the mistake, working an inning that on paper already ended, while the man who made it stays at his position.

The new field note is about a man being kept on after he was the problem: the job priced wrong that the company eats, the redo two other men stay late for, the thing said at the table that doesn't cost a man his place at it. It's also plain about the failures that aren't errors and shouldn't be treated as one. [canonical link]

# Substack Notes

## Note 1

In a scorebook every position has a number, and shortstop is six, so the record of a booted ground ball is efficient: E6. Two characters. Anybody in the park who can read a scorebook knows exactly who. In a men's league the official scorer is usually whoever remembered to bring a pencil.

## Note 2

Every man who's ever played believes the ball finds the guy who just made an error. Coaches believe it. Children believe it within two seasons. It has never once been a comfort to anybody.

## Note 3 — canonical link

Nothing that happens in the seventh reaches back into the third and rubs out a letter. The E stays under the shortstop's name, in pencil, for good. Next week's lineup card has him at short. [canonical link]
