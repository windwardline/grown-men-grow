# Ghost essay source

A garage door opener is about a third of a horsepower. That is a kitchen appliance. The door hanging under it weighs somewhere between a hundred and fifty and four hundred pounds, depending on whether it is steel or wood and whether anybody thought to insulate it.

Those two numbers do not go together, and a man can watch that door go up ten thousand times without the arithmetic ever once coming up.

The opener is not lifting the door. It has never lifted the door. What it does is overcome friction and settle an argument about direction. The lifting is done by a spring on a shaft above the opening — wound tight, painted the same colour as the ceiling, doing nothing that looks like anything.

A balanced door, halfway up with the opener disconnected, stays where it is. It does not fall and it does not rise. Two hundred pounds of steel standing still in the air, because something overhead is holding exactly as much as it weighs and not an ounce more.

## The loud part is not the working part

The spring makes no noise at all. The opener makes all of it — the chain slapping, the rail ringing, the whole assembly announcing itself up through the kitchen floor at twenty past six. It is the loudest thing in the building and it is doing the least work in it.

So when a door stops going up, the opener gets the blame. A bigger one goes in. Three-quarter horsepower, belt drive, quieter, an app. And the door still does not go up, because three quarters of a horse cannot lift two hundred pounds either, and the thing that actually let go is a two-inch gap in a spring nobody has looked at in nine years.

Nobody is being stupid here. A man troubleshoots the part he can hear.

## He can hear his own motor

He knows exactly what his effort sounds like. The early alarm is his. The hours are his. The thing he made himself do in February, when everything in him voted the other way, is entirely his, and he could produce the receipt for it on request.

So when the door goes up, he credits the motor. It is an honest mistake and close to the only one available to him, because the motor is the part with his name on it — the part he carried in from the truck, the part that answers when he pushes the button.

What is actually taking the weight does not announce itself, because announcing is not what it is for. Somebody rearranges her own year around a stretch of his and files it nowhere. A friend takes a call at a bad hour and says nothing about the hour, then or afterward. Two men at work move a date and give a different reason for moving it. His parents did arithmetic at a kitchen table in 1994 that he has not been shown and is not going to be. His own body runs a decade on a margin nobody asked him to authorise.

None of it makes a sound. A spring holding two hundred pounds off a concrete floor is silent, and that is not modesty. It is what a wound spring is. It has nothing to say and no mechanism for saying it.

## Nobody winds one gently

Winding a torsion spring is done by hand, a quarter turn at a time, with two steel bars, on a shaft at head height. It is the part of a garage door that puts people in the hospital, and every trade that touches it does so with a specific unsentimental respect.

It also does not get done precisely. A half turn heavy and the door will not stay down — it creeps up on its own an inch at a time overnight, and a door with opinions is not a better door. A half turn light and it comes down harder than anybody intended, and the whole house learns the sound.

So the care arrives mis-tensioned, because it is applied by people with bars in their hands and no gauge on anything. Somebody gives too much and will not let him carry what he is perfectly able to carry and would like to. Somebody gives too little and calls that respecting his independence, and is half right, which is the worst available amount to be. Somebody tells him what the winding cost — once, which is a fact, and then a second time, which is a different kind of sentence.

And springs get fitted wrong in a way that is nobody's fault and hard to see. A spring is sized in wire diameter and length for one particular door weight, and a counter will hand over what is on the shelf. Men mostly give the care they would have wanted, wound to their own door. It works. The door goes up. It is not the spring that door needed and it will not last the way the right one would have, and no one in the building is in a position to know that.

None of this is a job anybody enjoys. Fifteen years of paint on the set screws, a ladder in the way of the shaft, and a wound spring six inches from a man's hands the entire time.

## Most doors are hung on one spring

One spring where the weight calls for two is cheaper, and it works in the sense that matters to anybody standing in the driveway: the door goes up. What it does not do is fail politely. One spring has nothing beside it, so when it goes, all of it goes at once, and the door comes down the whole way.

A good number of men are running on one spring and could not name it. A good number are running on none — lifting the door by hand every morning of their lives, getting strong in the specific way a man gets strong lifting a two-hundred-pound door, with no particular reason to think doors work any other way.

## Ten thousand cycles

A torsion spring is rated in cycles, and the standard one is good for about ten thousand. That is seven years at the rate an ordinary household opens a door — work, back from work, the kid, the groceries, the thing he forgot. Every trip spends some of it. Nobody in the house is aware of spending anything.

There is no counter on it. Nothing on the wall reports the number, no light comes on at nine thousand, and the door in its last week goes up exactly like the door in its first.

The door goes up at whatever hour it goes up, in about twelve seconds. The motor is the only part of it anybody in the house can hear.
