This would be easier to explain if I could say I came to it through scholarship.

I didn’t.

I came to it because my life fell apart and most of what I believed about myself turned out to be less permanent than advertised.

Before that, I worked in consulting. I also served briefly as a U.S. Navy information professional officer. Both environments rewarded clear thinking, preparation, competence, and the ability to make sense of a messy situation.

Those are useful skills.

They also gave me sophisticated ways to avoid feelings I could not solve with a framework.

I knew how to talk about responsibility long before I understood what it meant to be changed by it. I could study a pattern, explain it, and offer a tidy account of how it worked. That did not mean I could recognize the pattern while I was busy living inside it.

The last several years dismantled most of the life I knew. A marriage ended. The career path disappeared. So did the home, the community, the income, and much of the reputation I had confused with identity.

Some of what happened was done to me. Some came from choices I made. Some refuses to fit cleanly into either category, which is inconvenient if you want a simple story with a clear villain.

There are public records. There is private grief. There are other people involved who did not consent to become characters in something I write online.

This publication will not become a courtroom.

I am not interested in building an audience by reopening every wound, prosecuting someone else, or asking strangers to decide whether I deserve redemption. I have wanted to be understood badly enough to know that the desire can become its own form of avoidance.

The more useful question is what came after.

What does a man do when the things he used to prove his worth are gone? What happens when competence cannot fix the loss? What is left when charm, work, anger, explanation, and being right no longer get him where he wants to go?

I started reading because I needed better language. Love, attachment, honesty, sex, shame, grief, and the emotional lives of men were all subjects I had strong opinions about and a thinner understanding of than I realized.

Some books gave me words. Others irritated me. The irritating ones were often the useful ones.

The reading matters, but this is not a book report. An idea has to survive contact with an actual life.

Grown Men Grow is not an argument for making men smaller. I still believe in strength, ambition, appetite, nerve, competence, and the satisfaction of being useful. I am interested in what happens when those qualities mature alongside the rest of a man instead of covering for whatever he refuses to face.

The writing will deal with strength, sex, shame, anger, loneliness, ambition, divorce, friendship, grief, repair, and the strange experience of realizing that the version of you who survived may not be the version capable of building a decent life.

I am not a therapist. I do not diagnose readers, offer treatment, or pretend my experience is universal. I am also not writing from the far side of some beautiful transformation.

I am still in it.

Some weeks I understand the work better than I live it. Some weeks I will probably write something I need to come back and complicate later. I would rather risk that than manufacture the voice of a man who has arrived.

A grown man is not a finished man.

He is simply old enough to become responsible for what he does with the unfinished parts.

That is what this publication is about.
