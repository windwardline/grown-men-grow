# Publication Naming Shortlist

Date: 2026-08-07

Status: Superseded by founder decision; retained as historical screening evidence. Current identity decisions live in `founder-decisions.md`.

## Final decision

The founder selected **Grown Men Grow**, registered `grownmengrow.com`, and claimed `@grownmengrow` on 2026-08-07. The shortlist below is retained as historical decision evidence.

## Standard

The name should:

- sound human rather than clinical or institutional;
- avoid guru, coaching, manosphere, grievance, and gender-war cues;
- leave room for uncertainty, practice, and lived reasoning;
- support essays now without limiting later formats;
- work for men and for people who care about men;
- remain distinct from the founder's legal and reputation history;
- pair naturally with the existing working phrase without turning that phrase into a finalized brand by default;
- support a credible `.com` domain and Instagram identity.

## Recommendation

### 1. A Man in Practice

Best fit for a founder-led publication.

Why it works:

- It describes an ongoing discipline rather than a finished identity.
- It positions the founder as a participant in the work, not an authority above it.
- It feels personal without becoming confessional.
- It is specific to men without claiming a universal definition of manhood.
- It can hold essays about strength, grief, desire, repair, truth, and accountability without narrowing the subject to one of them.

Main risk:

The word “practice” can sound professional or clinical in isolation. The complete phrase and publication presentation need to make its reflective meaning clear.

## Strong alternatives

### 2. What Strength Is For

Best fit for an idea-led publication.

Why it works:

- It turns the project's central concern into an inquiry rather than a doctrine.
- It welcomes readers who might resist a publication explicitly branded around masculinity.
- It gives strength a purpose and relationship rather than treating strength itself as the problem.
- It is memorable and can support a wider body of work.

Main risk:

Without a clear descriptor and visual identity, it could be mistaken for strength training, leadership, or performance content.

Preliminary identity check:

- `whatstrengthisfor.com`: no registration record returned on 2026-08-07.
- `@whatstrengthisfor`: exact Instagram availability not yet confirmed.

### 3. Manhood in Practice

Best fit if immediate category clarity matters most.

Why it works:

- The subject is unmistakable.
- “In practice” rejects abstract doctrine and completed mastery.
- It supports a publication, newsletter, or future book without depending on the founder's name.

Main risk:

It is more institutional than the preferred voice and may resemble a religious men's program or curriculum.

Preliminary identity check:

- `manhoodinpractice.com`: no registration record returned on 2026-08-07.
- `@manhoodinpractice`: exact Instagram availability not yet confirmed.

### 4. The Question Underneath

Best fit for a literary, inquiry-led publication.

Why it works:

- It reflects the project's commitment to better questions and thought unfolding in real time.
- It is restrained, memorable, and not self-congratulatory.
- It leaves room for the publication to grow without a platform change.

Main risk:

The subject of masculinity is invisible in the name. A descriptor would have to carry more explanatory weight.

Preliminary identity check:

- `thequestionunderneath.com`: no registration record returned on 2026-08-07.
- `@thequestionunderneath`: exact Instagram availability not yet confirmed.

### 5. The Quiet Work of Men

Best fit for a reflective field-note identity.

Why it works:

- It emphasizes unglamorous work rather than performance or applause.
- It feels like an essay publication rather than a program.
- It identifies the subject without offering a formula for “real men.”

Main risk:

“Quiet” could be misread as endorsing male silence, suppression, or invisibility, which conflicts with the project's commitment to honest expression. It is also long for repeated visual use.

## Reserve candidate

### A Man, Honestly

This is intimate, memorable, and voice-aligned, but it places unusual weight on the founder's truthfulness. Given the project's reputation context, that framing could pull attention toward credibility disputes instead of the work. Keep it in reserve rather than the final three.

## Names screened out

- **The Unfinished Man:** existing poetry title and current masculinity podcast usage.
- **After the Performance:** too easily read as theatre or performer-recovery content.
- **Becoming Trustworthy:** active use across religious, leadership, and men's content; sounds instructional.
- **Masculinity, Unperformed:** clear but cerebral, and likely to pull the publication into academic gender-performativity framing.
- **Strength Without Applause:** too close to generic motivational and resilience language.
- **One Careful Essay:** captures cadence but not subject or durable identity.

## Preliminary availability limits

The domain checks above are point-in-time registry lookups, not reservations. A missing `.com` record can change at any moment.

Search and registry screening are not trademark clearance. Before purchase and public use, the final two candidates need:

1. an exact domain recheck;
2. an in-app Instagram username check;
3. a broader publication, podcast, book, business, and social-name search;
4. a preliminary United States trademark search;
5. legal review if the founder wants formal clearance or intends to invest materially in the mark.

No domain or social account should be purchased, claimed, or renamed until the founder chooses the finalist.

## Historical recommended final round

At that stage, the recommendation was to advance these three:

1. **A Man in Practice** — founder-led and human.
2. **What Strength Is For** — idea-led and expansive.
3. **Manhood in Practice** — category-clear and durable.

The founder resolved the question by selecting the durable category brand **Grown Men Grow**.
