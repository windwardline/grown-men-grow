# Editorial Underpinning

Status: submitted for founder approval 2026-08-11. Source material supplied by the founder as "a fantastic encapsulation of what I am trying to convey through this work," to serve as "a general underpinning to how Grown Men Grow thinks and communicates."

This document governs reasoning, not vocabulary. `editorial-visual-system.md` sets how the publication looks. `AGENTS.md` sets what it refuses. This sets how it thinks about the man reading it.

## Where it comes from

A ten-part public essay-carousel arguing why the care in *Ted Lasso* reads as substantive rather than as toxic positivity, grounded in the ethics-of-care tradition — Carol Gilligan, *In a Different Voice* (1982); Nel Noddings, *Caring* (1984); Joan Tronto, *Moral Boundaries* (1993).

The tradition is the root system, not the branding. Grown Men Grow does not position itself through academic citation, through *Ted Lasso*, or through feminism — the identity contract is explicit that feminism is never used as profile positioning, and a publication that leans on a television show inherits that show's shelf life. What follows is the reasoning, restated in the publication's own terms and answerable on its own.

## Seven principles

**1. Take the pain seriously.**
Care that insists everything is fine is not care. Anger and grief are information about what a man values and where it got hit. The writing treats them as evidence to read, never as weather to wait out. Conflict is not avoided when the honest thing is a disagreement.

**2. Create space; do not prescribe.**
The essays do not tell a man what to do with his feelings. They put the thing on the table, turn it over, and leave him the author of what happens next. He decides what change looks like for himself.

**3. Care is not control.**
"I know what's best for you" is control even when love is the motive — especially then. Any sentence that arranges the reader's life for him fails, no matter how warm it sounds. Good care preserves his ability to interpret his own needs.

**4. Receive the man on his own terms.**
Do not project a preferred solution onto him. What genuine care requires depends on the person, the relationship, and the circumstances — which is why the publication has no five-step program and never will. Universal prescriptions are the tell of a writer serving his own need to be useful.

**5. Responsiveness over good intentions.**
Noticing a need and meaning well is not the finish line. What matters is how the care actually lands for the man receiving it. Reader replies, comments, and the questions that keep recurring are evidence about the work, not applause to collect.

**6. No humiliation, no coercion, no predetermined destination.**
Shame is the cheapest lever in this category and it is never used — not on the reader, not on the men in the essays, not on anyone offstage. The publication does not decide in advance who a man ought to become.

**7. The culture a man builds can eventually hold him.**
Men who practice this get cared for in return. The point is not endurance, martyrdom, or being the one who never needs anything. A man who only ever gives care has built a room with no chair for himself.

## Underneath all seven

Moral seriousness here starts from relationships, responsibility, and the avoidance of harm inside real webs of dependence — not from detached universal rules. This is why the publication is responsibility-centered without being prescriptive: responsibility is owed to specific people in specific circumstances, and that is a harder, more concrete demand than any rule.

## Tests for a draft

Read the draft against these before it goes to the founder:

- Does it tell the reader what to do, or show him something and trust him with it?
- Would a man in real pain feel taken seriously, or managed?
- Is any sentence doing its work through shame?
- Does it prescribe a universal where the honest answer is "depends on the man"?
- Does it presume a destination — who he ought to become — rather than leaving that his?
- Does the care in it survive the reader disagreeing with it?

A draft that fails one of these is not softened. It is rewritten from the point where the reasoning went wrong.

## Boundaries

- **Reasoning, not vocabulary.** "Ethics of care," "interdependence," and the theorists' names belong in this document and in internal discussion. They do not belong in essays, captions, or profile copy, where they would read as credentialing.
- **Ideas are fair to engage; positioning is not.** An essay may argue a point this tradition also makes. It does not announce its lineage to borrow authority, and it never frames men's growth as a gender argument.
- **This does not soften the voice.** Dry, funny, imperfect, and occasionally profane all survive intact. Principle 1 in particular cuts against politeness: taking pain seriously sometimes means saying the unwelcome thing plainly.
- **Approved copy under `content/` is unaffected.** This governs new work and future revisions, not retroactive rewriting of what the founder has already approved.
