# LinkedIn Page

Concrete sets in about two days and cures for twenty-eight. A slab poured Monday will hold a man's weight by Wednesday without complaint — it looks finished, and it is roughly a third of the way to the strength the engineer specified. The rest of the building is scheduled against the twenty-eight-day number, not against how the thing feels underfoot.

Set and cured are two different events.

The new field note is about what that distinction costs when nobody applies it to himself. The man who reports that he is fine six weeks after something took him apart is usually not performing — that reading insults him, and it is the lazy one. He is reporting accurately. The surface really is hard. He gets up, he goes in, he runs the meeting, and every test he knows how to run comes back passing. He is simply running a surface test on a question about depth, and nobody has handed him a second gauge.

The other half is the part that gets left out. A slab under cure is not left alone. Somebody covers it, somebody comes back and wets it down, somebody sets the tape and takes the irritation for it, for days, on behalf of something that already looks done. That is most of what care actually consists of — and for a lot of men it is the harder half to accept. [canonical link]

# Substack Notes

## Note 1

The word everyone uses is drying. That is not what happens. Concrete cures through a reaction that needs water to keep going, and a slab left to dry out fast in the sun stops gaining strength permanently, at whatever number it had reached when the water left. The instinct runs exactly backwards.

## Note 2

Load a slab early and it rarely fails on the spot. That is what makes it a bad decision instead of an obvious one. It takes the weight, everyone is relieved, and it takes on hairline cracks and a lower ceiling that nobody meets for years — until something ordinary lands far harder than it has any business landing.

## Note 3 — canonical link

Some men do not get the month. The job does not hold, the money says go. What is worth something there is knowing the load is early and the cracking is physics, not a verdict. [canonical link]
