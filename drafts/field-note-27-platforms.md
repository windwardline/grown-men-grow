# Bluesky

## Post 1

A key machine doesn't know which parts of the old key are the pattern and which parts are twenty years in somebody's pocket. It copies the rounding as faithfully as the cuts. By the third generation the key sticks.

## Post 2

Most men find out how they were cut when the milk goes over and a sentence leaves their mouth in their father's voice. Not the words. The pitch, the timing, the little sigh in front of it.

## Post 3 — canonical link

His father was a copy too. Most of what got copied is good, and some of it never should have been. Which cut is which is his to sort, or not. [canonical link]

# LinkedIn Page

A boy doesn't learn his father from instructions. He learns him from proximity: how a bill he couldn't pay got handled, what happened when somebody cried, whether apologies happened at all. He copies the good with the rest.

The new field note borrows a locksmith's distinction between a key copied from a worn copy and a key cut from the manufacturer's code. It's straight about the men whose fathers should never have been copied, and about the ones who had nobody to copy at all. [canonical link]
